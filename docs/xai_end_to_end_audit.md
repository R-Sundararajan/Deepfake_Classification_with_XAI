### Inference contract

Image checkpoints use FAKE=0, REAL=1; unified DFDC heads use REAL=0, FAKE=1. S0 explicitly reverses legacy output order. The app targets the predicted class using a FAKE probability threshold of 0.5, including its tie convention. Tests independently check both mappings. All inputs are RGB, bilinear resized to 224×224, then normalized by ImageNet mean (0.485, 0.456, 0.406) and std (0.229, 0.224, 0.225). Image champions use full-frame input; face-mode DFDC systems use the largest YuNet face with alignment and an explicit full-frame fallback.

Video input has eight distinct bin-center samples. Frozen encoders normally use CUDA FP16 autocast; explanations recompute the selected frame in FP32. The maximum clean prediction-versus-explanation target probability difference in these 14 runs was 4.661083e-05. This precision difference did not change a decision. It is a numerical approximation to deployed inference, not bitwise equivalence to its FP16 encoder path. Video temperature is included; image spatial fallbacks are uncalibrated.

## 3. XAI results

### Mathematical and rendering checks

- **Grad-CAM:** spatially average selected-score gradients, weight the final convolutional activation channels, sum, ReLU, then bilinear upsample. R18 activations are 1×512×7×7; EfficientNet activations are 1×1280×7×7. A separate reference calculation agreed within 5.97e-8. Coarse maps and blurred boundaries reflect this spatial resolution, not verified manipulation masks. See the [original Grad-CAM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.02391).
- **Grad-CAM++:** the code uses squared/cubed first gradients in its alpha approximation, positive gradients for channel weights, then ReLU. It is explicitly labeled an approximation; it does not compute exact higher derivatives through arbitrary temporal heads. Known-feature tests verify class separation and small-gradient retention. This audit does not claim equivalence to exact higher-order [Grad-CAM++](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11063).
- **Layer-CAM:** positive local gradients multiply activations before channel summation/ReLU. The implementation chooses the final layer only; it does not claim multiscale fusion or pixel-accurate localization. See the [authors’ LayerCAM project](https://mmcheng.net/layercam/).
- **IG:** Gauss–Legendre integration of selected logit/log-probability gradients along a straight path, multiplied by input minus baseline. Normalized zero means ImageNet mean RGB, not black. Signed channel summation is valid; sum of absolute channel values is a separate magnitude diagnostic. The theoretical [completeness property](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/sundararajan17a.html) concerns the unnormalized attribution sum. The app’s residual tolerance is an operational numerical heuristic, not a proof of convergence.
- **Occlusion:** replace exactly each nonoverlapping 32-pixel patch with normalized zero, compute clean target probability minus perturbed probability, and fill that patch with its delta. Both positive and negative deltas are retained. Patch bounds, raw probability statistics and target-versus-best-alternative margin deltas are now available together. Changing the display to margins would change its meaning; this audit did not do so. Mean-color masks can be out of distribution, as in the original [occlusion-based analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2901).
- **LIME:** 7×7 grid segmentation, 256 Bernoulli masks with an all-present first sample, normalized-zero replacements, cosine distance ×100, exponential kernel width 25, and the installed LimeBase weighted Ridge surrogate for the selected probability. This is a local grid surrogate rather than semantic superpixels. R² and local prediction are inspected; poor fits remain visible. See [LIME’s implementation](https://github.com/marcotcr/lime/blob/master/lime/lime_base.py).
- **Attention:** native DeiT attention matrices have 1×3×197×197 shape across 12 layers (one class token and a 14×14 patch grid). Head-mean attention gets identity residuals, row normalization, successive matrix products, then class-token-to-patch extraction. An independent rollout agreed exactly; class targets 0 and 1 gave identical raw rollout. Gradient attention uses positive attention×selected-score-gradient before residual rollout. Fused-attention flags and hooks are restored. These are descriptive, non-causal visualizations. See [attention rollout](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.385/).
- **Rendering:** signed -1/0/+1 maps to blue/white/red; unsigned maps use Inferno. Opacity scales with absolute attribution and zero attribution preserves original RGB exactly. Resizing aligns maps to the actual 224-pixel input, not the uncropped upload. Each method normalizes independently; colors do not compare confidence or absolute effect across methods. No face masks or cosmetic suppression were introduced.

### Confusing outputs and stability

`fake.jpg`: CAM/Layer-CAM place coarse support near the glasses/cheeks; IG is faint and textured. At 32/64/128 mean-baseline nodes, relative completeness residuals were 7.89% / 1.35% / 0.149%. The production adaptive path stops at 64, meeting its stated tolerance. Absolute channel magnitude and signed sum have different meanings; the ratio of sum(abs(signed spatial sum)) to sum(abs(channel contributions)) is 0.895. The faint overlay is not primarily a cancellation failure and was not sharpened.

`real.jpeg`: a central positive CAM coexists with predominantly negative probability-occlusion values and negative total REAL-logit IG versus the mean baseline. A predicted class can have lower raw logit than its baseline while still winning by its margin over the alternative. All 49 mean-patch masks slightly increased an already saturated REAL probability: deltas ranged from -3.10e-6 to -1.19e-6. Max normalization therefore makes a blue map from extremely small, correctly signed values. The arithmetic is expected; the new visible magnitude caption prevents effect-size confusion. Mean-baseline IG relative residuals at 32/64/128 were 61.8% / 2.82% / 9.11%; more nodes are not monotonically better on a piecewise network. The 64-node blur baseline gave 1.55%, but answers a different reference question. This is limited numerical stability, not evidence of a wrong formula.

`Goodies.jpeg`: probability occlusion and LIME are exactly constant at FP32 precision. Larger coordinate masks changed the REAL-versus-FAKE margin by -3.43 to -5.05 while probability remained 1.0. A constant probability map is valid under saturation and cannot establish model insensitivity. LIME R²=1 on a constant target does not establish spatial fidelity. Existing constant-map notices correctly warn that localization is unavailable.

Three cases failed the production IG tolerance at 128 nodes: Saved Pictures 2.mp4/S6, held-out 00001.jpg/R18, and aaqaifqrwn/S8. Their maps remain numerically inconclusive. Some video cases meet the mixed absolute/relative tolerance despite large relative residuals (for example S4: 42.1%); the 0.01 absolute allowance dominates small output differences. Inspect both residuals in research use. No integration settings were changed to make these maps look better.

LIME on fake.jpg has R²=0.316, and map correlations across seeds 42/43/44 were 0.169, 0.0965 and -0.122. real.jpeg is similarly unstable. These are demonstrably weak local surrogates under saturated and heavily masked predictions, not reliable semantic edit maps. The existing weak-fit warnings are appropriate. E02’s FAKE test example had R²=0.653; the S6 REAL held-out clip had R²=0.708. A better fit still does not prove causality.

E02’s maps on the labeled test faces emphasize borders/background more than R18’s maps. This difference is not itself a defect. On the FAKE test image, deleting the top 15% CAM pixels lowered target probability by 0.07559 versus 0.04450 for the mean of three random deletions (advantage 0.03110). A top-left 64-pixel square lowered it by 0.06188, and a 32-pixel top band by 0.03159. These probes support actual contextual sensitivity but also mask some hair/boundary content and introduce OOD edges; they do not isolate a natural-background shortcut.

Occlusion patch sizes 16/32/56, IG mean/blur baselines, fixed-node counts, signed/absolute raw arrays, and LIME seeds are recorded in experiments.json and local `arrays/*_ig_raw.npz`. [Signed versus absolute-magnitude diagnostic figure](../results/xai_audit_20261024/figures/ig_signed_vs_magnitude.png) visualizes both aggregations on the same raw 64-node attributions; absolute magnitude is not positive class evidence. Deleting the top 15% of each signed map was compared with three seeded random deletions; all negative advantages are retained. This is a small, OOD perturbation diagnostic, not an attribution quality benchmark.

## 4. Implementation findings and changes

### Confirmed diagnostics defect: probability methods mislabeled as logits

`inference/unified.py::ResearchPredictor.explain` unconditionally overwrote `score_space` with “selected class logit/log-probability”. Occlusion and LIME actually call softmax probabilities. Replaying the original committed function with a probability diagnostic reproduces the overwrite: [original_metadata_reproduction](../results/xai_audit_20261024/controls.json). The minimal fix preserves method-supplied score-space metadata and labels raw attention as class agnostic. `explainability/methods.py` now explicitly supplies probability score-space labels. The focused regression fails on the original function and passes with the fix. No score calculation or attribution map was changed.

### Saturation presentation improvement

`explainability/methods.py::occlusion_sensitivity` now records largest absolute probability effect, patch coordinates and independent target-margin drops. `app.py::show_explanation` displays the effect in percentage points and notes effects near FP32 resolution. This addresses the observed magnitude ambiguity without suppressing valid background or negative attributions. Original normalization, signs and colors are preserved. The resolution flag uses eight FP32 epsilons as a diagnostic heuristic, not a model confidence threshold. Cache version was incremented so cached old diagnostics are not mistaken for the new result.

## 5. Model-behavior findings

**Demonstrated:** R18 responds to small visible background side strips on fake.jpg. Replacing only x=[0,16) and x=[208,224), y=[16,176), while preserving the central head pixels, changed target FAKE probability from 0.9999865 to 0.9072822 (mean RGB), 0.9967788 (black), and 0.9987262 (white). Margin drops were 8.937 / 5.483 / 4.553. A wider outer-strip replacement gave a 31.16-percentage-point mean-color drop, but borders may include peripheral subject pixels; the conservative side strips are the stronger background-specific evidence. Repeated maps were identical.

**Interpretation:** this is reproducible background/context sensitivity under controlled replacement. It is a model limitation for interpretation, not an occlusion implementation defect. Hard replacement boundaries are OOD and can themselves drive changes; this evidence does not prove the training set contains a shortcut, nor does it establish failure on natural backgrounds. Establishing that would require realistic foreground segmentation/compositing across many labeled, independent subjects and sources.

The S4 selected-frame background-window mask changed the complete FAKE probability by 0.00679 (mean), 0.00135 (black), and 0.00377 (white). S8’s corresponding effects were about -0.00089 to -0.00096. Attention on window/ceiling regions therefore cannot be dismissed solely because it lies outside the face, but raw attention alone is not causal evidence. These are checkpoint-specific, frame-conditional observations.

Goodies.jpeg depicts desk items rather than a face. The full-frame image classifier assigns essentially 100% REAL to this out-of-domain input. Its authenticity label is unknown; this is not counted as an error or a correct classification. The confident output illustrates the absence of a validated abstention/OOD guarantee, not evidence that the pictured items were authentically generated.

S6/S8 attribution includes the selected LSTM decision rather than substituting the spatial frame score. Native differentiable gates agreed with cuDNN logits within 4.30e-5 in the S8 control. Selected and second-frame probability correspondence were checked while the cached context remained unchanged. Position-mean feature replacement and reversed-sequence scores are retained, without claiming a temporal anomaly detector. Neither high confidence nor correct labels proves correct reasoning or broad generalization. No weights were changed to address these findings.

## 7. Final verdict

1. **Do current XAI outputs behave as expected?** The tested calculations, target selections, signs, spatial correspondence and rendering are consistent with their stated methods. This is bounded evidence, not proof for all architectures and inputs.
2. **Unusual but valid?** Faint/textured IG, differences from coarse CAM, negative occlusion on a highly confident REAL prediction, saturation-flat probability maps, and class-agnostic background attention are mathematically plausible and observed. Weak LIME and failed-completeness IG remain inconclusive rather than validated explanations.
3. **Actual implementation defects?** Yes: unified-model probability-method diagnostics were mislabeled as logits. No reproducible attribution arithmetic defect was established.
4. **Fixed and verified?** Score-space metadata is preserved; occlusion now exposes effect magnitude and margin diagnostics. Focused regression, full numerical reruns and affected browser upload workflows verify the change. Classification scores and attribution arrays remain exactly unchanged.
5. **Problematic learned behavior?** Background replacement sensitivity and saturated probabilities are reproducible. They warrant caution about contextual dependence and explanation fidelity. Natural-background shortcut exploitation, calibration failure on a population, and broad unreliability were not established by this cohort.
6. **Inconclusive?** Four Saved Pictures labels; several IG integrations; weak/seed-unstable LIME fits; exact higher-order Grad-CAM++ equivalence; causal interpretation of attention; untested catalog variants; external/actor-disjoint generalization. Additional independently labeled sources, realistic background controls, integration convergence studies and broader surrogate stability tests are needed.
7. **Ready for a defensible research demonstration?** **Yes, as a limited, transparently qualified model-behavior demonstration using verified examples and visible diagnostics.** It is not ready to support claims that bright regions locate verified manipulations, that all methods are faithful on every sample, or that this audit establishes general forensic reliability. Present failed/inconclusive explanations alongside successful ones.
